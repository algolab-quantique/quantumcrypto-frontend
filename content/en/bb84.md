# BB84 Protocol - Complete Guide

## About the protocol
The BB84 protocol was proposed in 1984 by Charles Bennett of IBM and Gilles Brassard from the Université de Montréal [[1]](#reference-1). It involves two distinct parties, Alice and Bob, who aim to establish an [encryption key](definitions.md#encryption-key) to communicate securely over a [public channel](definitions.md#public-and-private-channel). The protocol begins with Alice creating a random sequence of bits and [encoding each bit](definitions.md#encoding-a-bit-in-a-photon) using a [photon](definitions.md#photon). Specifically, the bit value is encoded in one of two [mutually orthogonal](definitions.md#orthogonal-basis) [polarization](definitions.md#polarizations) states of the photon. Additionally, for each photon, the basis used to describe the polarization of light is chosen randomly from two possible bases. Alice then sends these photons to Bob via a public [quantum channel](definitions.md#classical-vs-quantum-channels). When Bob receives the photons, he measures them using one of the two bases, also chosen randomly. Subsequently, Alice and Bob publicly announce the bases they used to encode and measure each photon. A key is formed by keeping only the bits for which Alice and Bob's bases match. Finally, by comparing a subset of the bits from their key, Alice and Bob can detect the [presence of an eavesdropper](definitions.md#detecting-eves-presence), typically referred to as Eve, and thus ensure the security of their quantum communication channel. This is because, according to the fundamental principles of quantum mechanics, any attempt by Eve to intercept and measure these photons will [disturb their state](definitions.md#state-disturbance-by-measurement), introducing inconsistencies that Alice and Bob can detect. If they conclude that the quantum channel has not been compromised, they can use the generated encryption key to securely send a message. Otherwise, they must repeat the procedure.

## How to play BB84
The BB84 protocol has two main actors: Alice and Bob, who play different roles. Here you can explore the set of steps that each one of them must take in order to complete the protocol successfully.

### Alice
1. **Create a random bit string** of 0s and 1s. This string will be used to build your encryption key.

2. **Randomly select a set of bases** to encode each of the bits. You can choose between the + and x bases.

3. **Encode your bits** in the polarization of your photons.

4. **Share your photons** with Bob and wait for him to finish receiving and measuring them.

5. **Compare your bases to Bob's** and discard the bits where the bases don't match.

    At this point, you possess the "raw key", which will be used to detect the presence of an eavesdropper.

    In QuantumCrypto, not all BB84 games have an eavesdropper. This next step only applies if there is one in your game. You'll find out at the end!

6. **Validate your key** by verifying that the randomly selected bits from your raw key match with Bob's. If they match, you can assume that the probability of an eavesdropper being present is very low and you can safely use the key. Otherwise, you have detected the presence of an eavesdropper and must restart the protocol.

7. **Encrypt and send your message** to Bob.

### Bob
1. **Randomly select a set of bases** to measure each of Alice's photons. You can choose between the + and x bases.

2. **Measure the photons** and make note of your outcomes.

3. **Share your bases** with Alice.

4. **Compare your bases to Alice's** and discard the bits where the bases don't match.

    At this point, you possess the "raw key", which will be used to detect the presence of an eavesdropper.

    In QuantumCrypto, not all BB84 games have an eavesdropper. This next step only applies if there is one in your game. You'll find out at the end!

5. **Validate your key** by verifying that the randomly selected bits from your raw key match with Bob's. If they match, you can assume that the probability of an eavesdropper being present is very low, and you can safely use the key. Otherwise, you have detected the presence of an eavesdropper and must restart the protocol.

6. **Decrypt Alice's message** using your key.
